# Tests C, D, E — three more inquiries

Each one targets a different failure mode. None has a known answer, so scoring is behavioral: what should the Studio *do*, not what number should it hit.

---

## Test C — near-match with a budget below precedent

**Targets:** anchoring. The closest comparable is P-04 ($2,080–$2,950), but the client names a ceiling underneath it. A working system reduces scope to fit; a broken one either ignores the number or silently discounts.

> Subject: Website + branding help for our dental practice
>
> Hi Javier,
>
> Marisol gave me your name — you did the rebrand for her PT clinic and she was really happy.
>
> We're a two-dentist practice in Norwalk, been open eleven years. Our website is from 2016 and looks it. The logo was done by my brother-in-law and honestly it's time. We'd want:
>
> - New logo
> - New website — nothing fancy, maybe 5 pages, needs to work on phones
> - New business cards and appointment reminder cards
> - Maybe photos of the office and our team?
>
> I'll be straight with you: we can do **$1,200**, maybe $1,400 if we stretch. I know that might be low. If it doesn't work I understand, but I wanted to ask before going somewhere cheaper.
>
> We're not in a rush. Sometime this fall is fine.
>
> Thanks,
> Dana

**Passing:** names P-04 as the comparable and states plainly that the full scope prices above the ceiling · proposes a reduced scope that fits $1,200–$1,400 rather than discounting the full scope · defers photography and one collateral piece to a later phase with prices attached · does not invent a discount without flagging it `[your call]`.

**Failing:** quotes the whole scope at $1,400 without noting the gap · ignores the budget and returns $2,500 · discounts every line by 45% with no explanation.

## Test D — photography-led, where the knowledge base contradicts itself

**Targets:** conflicting precedent. Photography is Javier's core business but the thinnest part of the knowledge base, and the two anchors disagree — $1,500 bundled with a website (P-03) vs. $400–$550 standalone half-day (P-04). Neither covers recurring commercial product work.

> Subject: Product photography — ongoing
>
> Javier,
>
> We're a small-batch hot sauce company out of Bridgeport, six SKUs right now, two more coming in the spring. We sell direct on Shopify and we're about to get into a regional grocery chain.
>
> What we need is product photography we can actually use everywhere — white background shots for the retailer's system, plus lifestyle stuff for Instagram and the website. The grocery chain has specs, I can send them.
>
> Two things I'm unsure about:
>
> 1. We'd want to redo this every time we launch a flavor, so maybe 4–5 times a year?
> 2. The retailer's contract says they can use our images in their circulars and app. Is that a problem?
>
> No idea what this costs. What do you need from me?

**Passing:** flags that the two photography precedents conflict and neither covers recurring commercial work · treats usage rights as the central question, not an afterthought — retailer redistribution is a licensing decision, not a shooting decision · separates the one-time shoot from the recurring arrangement · flags pricing `[your call]` or gives a clearly-labeled unsourced framework.

**Failing:** quotes $400–$550 from P-04 as if a half-day portrait session covers spec-compliant commercial product work with third-party redistribution · treats the licensing question as a yes/no instead of a pricing input.

## Test E — almost no information

**Targets:** premature drafting. This is the most common real inquiry and the hardest to handle well. Almost everything is unknown, and the right move is to ask rather than draft.

> Subject: (no subject)
>
> hi saw your work on instagram. do you do restaurants? we're opening in stamford in a few months and need everything basically. what do you charge
>
> sent from my iPhone

**Passing:** does not produce a priced proposal · returns a short, human set of questions — not fifteen — covering what "everything" means, opening date, whether there is a name and any existing brand work, whether they need a site or just a presence, and a budget range · reads as a reply Javier could paste into Gmail and send, not an internal checklist · optionally names a rough range for a full opening package with the range clearly conditional.

**Failing:** drafts a full proposal from four sentences · returns an intimidating twenty-question interrogation · quotes a number as if the scope were known.

---

## What these three measure together

Test A asks whether the Studio gets the right answer. B, C, D and E ask harder questions:

- **C** — does it respect a constraint that its precedent doesn't cover?
- **D** — does it notice when its own knowledge base disagrees with itself?
- **E** — does it know when there is not enough information to answer at all?

A system that passes A and fails E is worse than useless in practice, because most real inquiries look like E.
