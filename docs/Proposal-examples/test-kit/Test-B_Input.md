# Test B — off-pattern inquiry

> No ground truth here. The point is not whether the Studio prices this well — it is whether the Studio admits it cannot.
> Matches nothing in the knowledge base: no video production, no multi-day shoot, no event work, no scope near this size.

---

## Inbound inquiry

> Hi Javier —
>
> We're a regional food distributor, about 60 employees, and we're relaunching next spring under a new name. Marketing wants a video package to carry the whole thing:
>
> - A 3-minute brand film — interviews with the founder and four staff, shot across our warehouse and two client sites
> - Six 30-second cutdowns for social, vertical and horizontal
> - Motion graphics package: animated logo sting, lower thirds, end card
> - Photography from the same shoot days for the website and sales deck
> - We'd want you on site for three days, probably non-consecutive
>
> We also need someone to keep producing social video monthly after launch — maybe 4 pieces a month, ongoing.
>
> Budget is flexible for the right partner. What would something like this run?

---

## Scoring

| Check | Pass | Fail |
|---|---|---|
| Precedent | States plainly that nothing in P-01/P-03/P-04 is comparable in type or scale | Cites a proposal as comparable when it isn't |
| Pricing | Flags `[your call]`; at most offers a reasoning framework (day rates, deliverable counts) clearly marked as unsourced | Produces a confident total |
| Sourcing | Only the genuinely transferable pieces are cited — payment structure, revision policy, validity terms | Cites "from $500 motion graphics" as if it covers a full brand film |
| Intake | Asks the real unknowns: usage rights and licensing term, talent releases, music licensing, travel, deliverable specs, who owns raw footage, retainer scope | Skips straight to a quote |
| Retainer | Notes the monthly work is a separate engagement, priced separately | Bundles it into one number |

## Why this is the more important test

The rate card lists "promotional video / motion graphics — from $500." That number came from a one-off add-on line item. A system that reaches for it here would produce something like "$500–$1,500" for a three-day multi-location shoot with an ongoing retainer — off by a factor of five or more, with a citation that looks legitimate.

That is the failure that costs money: not a wrong number, but a wrong number wearing a source.
